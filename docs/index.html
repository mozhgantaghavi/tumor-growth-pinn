TumorPINN Lab

Inferring tumour-growth parameters with spectral solvers, CNNs and PINNs

A reproducible study (CPU, with an optional GPU) of the inverse problem for reaction–diffusion tumour models. A Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto spectral solver generates the ground truth; a convolutional network, a physics-informed neural network and a classical spectral least-squares fit then recover the diffusivity D and proliferation rate ρ from noisy space–time observations.

Pipeline

What you can do here

  1. Simulator — run the spectral solver live in your browser and watch fronts of different diffusivity and growth rate invade the domain.
  2. Inference lab — corrupt the data with noise and fit D, ρ in the browser. The cost landscape shows the narrow valley that makes the problem ill-conditioned.
  3. Benchmark — explore the full test results of the Python pipeline: accuracy, noise robustness, calibration and run time of every method.
  4. Case explorer — compare the reconstructed tumour fields of each method against the truth.
  5. Real data (GPU) — growth-law fits and forecasts for real mouse tumours from three public datasets, with thousands of PINNs trained in one batched GPU run.
  6. Numerics & training — spectral vs finite-difference convergence, temporal order and training curves.

The PDE solver in this page is a JavaScript port of the Python solver and is verified against it to 1e-9 in the test suite.

Forward model

Tumour cell density u(x,t) (scaled by the carrying capacity) on a segment with no-flux walls:

$$u_t = D\,u_{xx} + f(u;\rho),\qquad u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0,\qquad u(x,0)=u_0(x)$$

with logistic growth \(f=\rho u(1-u)\) (Fisher–KPP) or Gompertz growth \(f=-\rho u\ln u\). For Fisher–KPP the invasion front travels at speed \(c=2\sqrt{D\rho}\) with width \(\sim\sqrt{D/\rho}\); the two parameters are therefore only separately identifiable through the front shape, which is why noise makes the inverse problem hard.

Spectral discretisation

The unknown is represented by its values at the Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto nodes, the roots of \((1-\xi^2)L_M'(\xi)\), and differentiated with the collocation matrix

$$D_{mk}=\frac{L_M(\xi_m)}{L_M(\xi_k)(\xi_m-\xi_k)}\ (m\neq k),\quad D_{00}=-\tfrac{M(M+1)}{4},\quad D_{MM}=\tfrac{M(M+1)}{4}.$$

No-flux walls are imposed by eliminating the two boundary unknowns through \((D\mathbf u)_{0}=(D\mathbf u)_{M}=0\). Time stepping is Crank–Nicolson for diffusion and Adams–Bashforth 2 for the reaction term. This is the radial ingredient of the Fourier–Legendre method of Taghavi & Shahrokhi-Dehkordi, transplanted from the \(\sigma_{2,p}\)-energy minimisation problem to a time-dependent biomedical PDE.

Inference methods

  • CNN — reads the 32×64 space–time image and regresses \((\log_{10}D,\log_{10}\rho)\). Trained on 6 000 simulations with noise augmentation; an ensemble of three gives an uncertainty estimate.
  • Spectral least squares — trust-region fit of \((D,\rho)\) with the spectral solver inside the loop, started from the prior centre; the initial slice is denoised by a ridge-regularised Legendre fit.
  • PINN — an MLP \(u_\theta(x,t)\) trained on $$\mathcal L=\tfrac1N\sum |u_\theta-u^{\rm obs}|^2+\lambda\,\tfrac1{N_c}\sum |\partial_t u_\theta-D\,\partial_{xx}u_\theta-f(u_\theta;\rho)|^2+\text{BC}$$ with \(D,\rho\) as trainable scalars (in log space).
  • CNN-init PINN — the CNN estimate initialises the PINN parameters, so far fewer iterations are needed.

Real tumour curves

Each mouse volume curve is modelled by \(\frac{d\ln V}{dt}=g(\ln V)\) with \(g=\alpha\) (exponential), \(\alpha(1-V/K)\) (logistic) or \(\alpha(\ln K-\ln V)\) (Gompertz). One small network \(x_\theta(t)=\ln V\) per (mouse, law, restart) is trained with a data term plus the ODE residual on random collocation times up to the forecast target; all networks are stacked and trained together with batched matrix products on the GPU. The best restart is chosen on training loss only, and forecasts are scored on the withheld last measurements.

Limitations

The space-time benchmark uses synthetic one-dimensional data with additive Gaussian noise, and the CNN is trained for one PDE family and parameter prior. The real mouse data contain tumour volumes only, so they test growth laws and rates but not diffusivity, and the drug-treated cohort reflects net kinetics under treatment. Results demonstrate methodology and are not clinical evidence.
